## 1. PLAYER EXPERIENCE

### 1.1 Complete Player Journey

```
MINUTE 0 — FIRST LAUNCH
─────────────────────────
The screen fades from black to a drone shot soaring over a small coastal city
at golden hour. An orchestral swell peaks, then fades to a quiet piano theme.
The title "INSTABUILT: BLUEPRINT EMPIRE" appears in clean white text over the
skyline. A single prompt: "Press Any Key to Begin."

After pressing, a character appears: MENTOR PLLANA, a warm but sharp man in
his early 60s, wearing a hard hat and a half-unbuttoned work shirt. He speaks
directly to the player:

    "You've got a truck, a mixer, and a plot of land your uncle left you.
    Most people see dirt. You're going to see a building. Let's get started."

No menu. No settings screen. The game begins.
```

#### PHASE 1: THE FIRST BUILD (Minutes 0-45)

**Emotional State:** Curiosity → mild frustration → breakthrough → pride.

The player is placed on an empty residential plot. Mentor Pllana stands beside them in first-person mode. He walks the player through:

1. **Viewing the plot** — "This is 450 square meters. Nice flat grade. Good soil. Your uncle had an eye for land."
2. **Opening the design tool** — A clean blueprint overlay appears. Mentor Pllana places the first wall. "Your turn."
3. **Designing a simple one-bedroom home** — The player drags walls, places a door, adds two windows. The design tool auto-validates ("Wall too thin for load-bearing — let me fix that").
4. **Ordering materials** — A simple material palette: foundation concrete, timber frame, drywall, roof tiles. Mentor Pllana explains each choice with one sentence.
5. **Hiring the first crew** — Two laborers arrive in a pickup truck. They have names (Marku and Arta). They wave.
6. **Starting construction** — The player clicks "Begin Construction." Time advances. The crew starts digging the foundation.
7. **Experiencing a minor problem** — Rain is forecast for tomorrow. Mentor Pllana: "Better cover that foundation before the storm hits, or we're redoing it." The player clicks "Tarp the Foundation." The crew deploys tarps. Rain comes. Foundation is saved.
8. **Watching the build progress** — Over ~15 real minutes (representing 6 in-game weeks), the house goes from foundation → frame → walls → roof → interior finishes.
9. **Final walkthrough** — First-person mode. The player walks through the completed house. Sunlight streams through the windows they placed. The floor creaks authentically. Mentor Pllana: "Not bad, kid. Not bad at all."
10. **Client handover** — A young couple arrives. They explore the house. The wife tears up. "It's perfect." The player receives their first payment: $185,000.

**Contract Complete.** The game opens the Company Dashboard for the first time.

#### PHASE 2: FINDING YOUR FOOTING (Hours 1-8)

**Emotional State:** Confidence through competence.

The player now has:
- A company (needs naming — default: "InstaBuilt Local")
- $185,000 in the bank
- One 5-star review
- Three new contract offers on the dashboard
- A reputation score of 15/100

The next 7 hours are spent completing 8-12 small contracts: garages, home extensions, another single-family home, a small retail renovation. Each contract teaches one new system:

- Contract #2: Multi-room design
- Contract #3: Client preferences (they want "modern farmhouse")
- Contract #4: Permits (the city requires a zoning variance)
- Contract #5: Loans (this project is bigger than your cash reserves)
- Contract #6: Subcontractors (you need an electrician — hire one)
- Contract #7: Material quality tiers (budget vs. standard vs. premium)
- Contract #8: Weather management (building through winter)

By hour 8, the player has 2 crews, 8 completed buildings in their portfolio, and Tier 2 unlock is visible on the horizon.

#### PHASE 3: SCALING UP (Hours 8-25)

**Emotional State:** Ambition meets complexity.

Tier 2 unlocks. The player can now build multi-family housing and POP UP 28 m² units. The game introduces:

- Multiple simultaneous projects
- The Architect role (first specialized hire)
- The full Business Dashboard (P&L statements, cash flow projections, tax events)
- Regional reputation tracking
- Equipment purchasing (first excavator purchase is a milestone moment)
- The Safety system (first minor incident: worker slips, sprains ankle — reminder that construction is physical)

The player designs their first apartment building. 6 units. More complex than anything before. The design tool now offers multi-floor views, stair placement, unit numbering. Construction takes 3 in-game months. When complete, six families move in. The player now has tenants — people living in spaces they created.

#### PHASE 4: BECOMING A DEVELOPER (Hours 25-50)

**Emotional State:** Mastery and ownership.

Tier 3-4. The player is now a recognized city developer. They:

- Bid against AI competitors for major contracts
- Have a named company headquarters (designable, naturally)
- Manage 3-5 active construction sites simultaneously
- Navigate the City Council (rezoning hearings, community impact reviews)
- Unlock Signature Homes — premium custom builds for wealthy clients with elaborate briefs
- Build their first high-rise
- Experience their first economic downturn (recession cuts new contracts by 40% for 6 months — adapt or struggle)
- See their buildings literally change the skyline

The emotional peak of this phase: The player flies the drone camera over the city at sunset and sees 30+ buildings — their buildings — forming entire neighborhoods. NPCs walk dogs on sidewalks the player's crews poured. Children play in a park built as a pro-bono community project.

#### PHASE 5: THE EMPIRE (Hours 50-80+)

**Emotional State:** Pride, nostalgia, creative freedom, legacy.

Tier 5-6. The player is a national or global construction powerhouse. The game shifts:

- Most site-level construction is delegated to project managers
- Player focus shifts to strategy: which cities to enter, which mega-projects to pursue
- The player designs their corporate headquarters — the ultimate creative project
- Legacy Milestones become the driving goal
- The player can revisit any building they've ever constructed, walk through it in first-person, and see the plaque on the wall: "Built by [Player Name] — [Year]"

The final emotional beat: Achieving the last Legacy Milestone triggers a cinematic. The camera pulls back from the player's first-ever garage build, rises through the city, passes every building they constructed (each briefly highlighted with its completion date), and settles on the skyline — their skyline. Mentor Pllana's voice, older now: "Your uncle would be proud. I know I am."

The game continues infinitely. Players can keep building, keep expanding, try Scenario Mode, or start a new career with a completely different specialization.

## 2. CAMERA SYSTEM

### 2.1 Camera Mode Overview

The game provides seven distinct camera modes. The player can switch between them at any time. Each mode serves a specific purpose.

### 2.2 Default Camera — "Orbit Mode"

**What the player sees:**
A top-down isometric-adjacent view at approximately 45° angle, positioned 20-80 meters above the active construction site. The camera orbits around a focal point the player sets by clicking on any object or location.

**What the player can do:**
- Scroll wheel: Zoom in/out (range: 5m above ground to 500m aerial)
- Right-click + drag: Orbit around focal point
- Middle-click + drag: Pan the focal point
- Double-click on any building/object: Set as new focal point
- Press "F": Focus camera on selected object

**When available:** Always. This is the primary gameplay camera.

**Why this design:** The isometric-adjacent angle provides clear spatial understanding for construction placement while maintaining a professional, architectural visualization aesthetic. The orbit mechanic gives the player full control without complexity.

**Pillars supported:** Build (primary), Design (secondary — good for overview)

### 2.3 Free Camera

**What the player sees:**
Unconstrained movement in 3D space. No focal point. No orbit limits.

**What the player can do:**
- WASD: Move camera forward/back/left/right
- Q/E: Move camera up/down
- Mouse: Look direction
- Shift: Speed boost
- Scroll: Adjust movement speed

**When available:** Always. Toggle with "V" key or camera mode button.

**Why this design:** Essential for screenshot composition, exploring completed buildings from any angle, and getting precise views for detailed design work. Power users live in this mode.

**Pillars supported:** Design (primary), Grow (portfolio screenshots)

### 2.4 First-Person Mode

**What the player sees:**
Eye-level view from approximately 1.7m height. Realistic field of view (default 90°). The player "is" on the construction site.

**What the player can do:**
- Mouse: Look around
- WASD: Walk (realistic walking speed ~5 km/h)
- Shift: Run (~12 km/h)
- Space: Jump (small hop — not a platformer)
- E: Interact with nearby objects/workers
- Scroll: Not applicable
- Right-click: Return to orbit mode at current position

**When available:** Always. Toggle with "F1" or character icon.

**Special interactions only in first-person:**
- Talk to workers directly (face-to-face dialogue wheel appears)
- Inspect construction quality up close (cracks, alignment issues visible)
- Client walkthroughs (required for final inspection on premium contracts)
- Read documents on desks in your office
- Experience weather viscerally (rain on screen, fogging, wind audio)

**Why this design:** The emotional payoff of construction is seeing it from human scale. Walking through a completed building you designed creates a moment of pride that top-down views cannot deliver. The first time a player walks through their first house is a core memory of the game.

**Pillars supported:** Build (inspection), Design (walkthrough validation), Grow (emotional payoff)

### 2.5 Drone Mode

**What the player sees:**
A small camera drone flies above the site. Slight lateral drift for realism. On-screen HUD shows altitude, speed, battery life (infinite — drone is for gameplay, not simulation), and GPS coordinates.

**What the player can do:**
- WASD: Fly drone horizontally
- Q/E: Ascend/descend
- Mouse: Tilt camera angle
- Scroll: Zoom camera lens (optical zoom, 1x-8x)
- Space: Take photo (saved to portfolio)
- R: Record video clip (30 seconds, saved to replays)
- Right-click: Return drone to home position

**When available:** Tier 2+. Unlocked by purchasing the "Survey Drone" equipment item ($8,500).

**Why this design:** Drones are real construction tools. This mode makes site surveying immersive and authentic. The photo/video functionality feeds directly into the portfolio and marketing systems. Players share drone shots on social media — free marketing for the game.

**Pillars supported:** Build (site surveying), Grow (portfolio, marketing)

### 2.6 Building Interior View

**What the player sees:**
Inside any building with completed interior walls. Walls become transparent when they block the camera view (configurable opacity). Furniture and fixtures render at full detail. Room labels appear floating at ceiling height ("Kitchen — 18.5 m²").

**What the player can do:**
- Same as Orbit Mode, but boundaries are room walls
- Click on rooms to jump camera to that room
- Click on furniture/fixtures to inspect specifications
- Toggle wall transparency with "T"
- Toggle furniture visibility with "G" (for checking floor plans)

**When available:** Any completed or in-progress building with walls. Auto-activates when zooming close enough to a building interior.

**Why this design:** Designing interiors is as important as exteriors. Players need to validate room flow, check fixture placement, and experience the space they created. Wall transparency solves the classic "camera outside the dollhouse" problem elegantly.

**Pillars supported:** Design (primary), Build (quality inspection)

### 2.7 Cinematic Camera

**What the player sees:**
Pre-set cinematic angles that slowly orbit or track along a path. No HUD. Letterbox bars (configurable). Depth of field effects.

**What the player can do:**
- 1-5: Select pre-set cinematic angles (overhead orbit, ground-level tracking, drone fly-by, time-lapse position, client arrival angle)
- Space: Capture screenshot at current frame
- Any other input: Exit cinematic mode

**When available:** Any time the player has completed at least one building. Accessible from the photo mode menu.

**Why this design:** Portfolio building and social sharing are core to long-term engagement. Cinematic tools make every player a potential content creator. A beautiful shot of your skyscraper at sunset is the best marketing the game can have.

**Pillars supported:** Grow (portfolio), Design (appreciation)

### 2.8 Construction Site Camera

**What the player sees:**
Fixed-angle camera positioned at one of 6 pre-set points around the active construction site. Overlaid with construction data: crew positions (colored dots), material stockpile levels (bars), active work zones (highlighted areas), weather forecast (corner widget).

**What the player can do:**
- 1-6: Jump between pre-set site cameras
- Hover over crew dot: See worker name, current task, fatigue level
- Hover over zone: See task description, % complete, assigned crew
- Click on zone: Issue priority command ("Focus here first")

**When available:** During active construction phase of any project.

**Why this design:** Construction management at scale requires overview. This camera mode transforms the site from a visual scene into a management interface without losing the visual connection to the work being done.

**Pillars supported:** Build (primary), Manage (secondary)

## 3. PLAYER CONTROLS

### 3.1 Design Philosophy

Controls follow three principles:

1. **Discoverability:** Common actions have visible UI buttons. Advanced actions have keyboard shortcuts revealed through tooltips. Nothing essential is hidden.
2. **Consistency:** The same action (select, move, delete) works identically across all game modes. Players build muscle memory.
3. **Forgiveness:** Undo is always available (up to 50 actions). Destructive actions require confirmation. Nothing is permanently lost.

### 3.2 Interaction Categories

#### Selection

| Action | Behavior |
|--------|----------|
| Left-click on object | Select object. Highlight outline appears. Info panel opens. |
| Left-click on empty space | Deselect all. |
| Left-click + drag (rectangle) | Select all objects within rectangle. |
| Shift + click | Add to current selection. |
| Ctrl + click | Toggle selection of clicked object. |
| Double-click on building | Select building and enter Building Interior View. |
| Right-click on object | Open context menu with object-specific actions. |

**Why this design:** Standard RTS-style selection that most players already know. Multi-select with rectangle is essential for batch operations (move all these walls 2 meters right). Context menu on right-click keeps the UI clean while providing discoverable actions.

**Pillars supported:** All.

#### Construction Placement

| Action | Behavior |
|--------|----------|
| Select tool from toolbar | Cursor changes to placement preview. |
| Move mouse over valid location | Preview snaps to grid and shows ghost of object in green. |
| Move mouse over invalid location | Preview shows ghost in red. Tooltip explains why (e.g., "Collides with existing wall"). |
| Left-click | Place object. Preview becomes real. Cost deducted. |
| Right-click during placement | Cancel placement. Return to selection mode. |
| Hold Shift during placement | Toggle snapping (free placement mode for fine adjustment). |
| Scroll during placement | Rotate object (for rotatable objects like furniture). |
| Ctrl+Z | Undo last placement. Full refund. |
| Ctrl+Shift+Z | Redo. |

**Why this design:** The green/red ghost preview eliminates placement guesswork. The tooltip on invalid placement teaches the player the rules naturally rather than through a tutorial. Snapping toggle gives precision when needed but keeps placement fast by default.

**Pillars supported:** Design (primary), Build (secondary).

#### Building Editing

| Action | Behavior |
|--------|----------|
| Select wall/room/object | Handles appear at edges/vertices. |
| Drag handle | Resize or reshape object. Real-time dimensions displayed. |
| Click dimension label | Enter exact measurement via number input. |
| Delete key | Delete selected object(s). Confirmation dialog with cost refund shown. |
| Ctrl+C | Copy selected object(s) to clipboard. |
| Ctrl+V | Paste copied object(s). Preview follows cursor. Click to place. |
| Ctrl+D | Duplicate selected object(s) in place (offset by 2m). |

**Why this design:** Handle-based editing is the standard for creative tools (Blender, SketchUp, The Sims). Dimension labels with direct input serve both casual players (drag to approximate) and precision players (type exact measurements). Copy/paste dramatically speeds up repetitive design work (placing 20 identical windows).

**Pillars supported:** Design (primary).

#### Inspection Mode

| Action | Behavior |
|--------|----------|
| Press "I" or click magnifying glass | Enter inspection mode. Cursor becomes magnifying glass. |
| Hover over any object | Detailed info tooltip appears: material, cost, install date, condition, maintenance history. |
| Hover over worker | Worker info card: name, role, fatigue, morale, current task, efficiency %. |
| Hover over construction zone | Zone info: task name, crew assigned, % complete, estimated time remaining, quality score. |
| Click while inspecting | Pin info card to screen (persists after moving cursor). Click pin to close. |
| Press "I" or Escape | Exit inspection mode. |

**Why this design:** Inspection is the primary "read the game state" interaction. Making it a mode (rather than a constant overlay) keeps the screen clean during normal gameplay while providing deep information on demand. Pinning allows comparing multiple items.

**Pillars supported:** Build (quality), Manage (worker/zone status).

#### Worker Interaction

| Action | Behavior |
|--------|----------|
| Left-click worker (orbit mode) | Select worker. Quick actions appear: Assign to Task, Send on Break, Promote, Dismiss. |
| Right-click worker | Full context menu with all worker actions. |
| Click worker in first-person | Opens dialogue wheel: "How's the job going?", "I'm promoting you.", "Take the rest of the day off.", "We need to talk about your performance." |
| Drag worker portrait to construction zone | Assign worker to that zone. |

**Why this design:** Workers are characters, not resources. Face-to-face interaction in first-person reinforces this. The quick action bar provides efficient management for experienced players. The dialogue wheel creates memorable character moments.

**Pillars supported:** Manage (primary), Build (assignment).

#### Vehicle Interaction

| Action | Behavior |
|--------|----------|
| Left-click vehicle (orbit mode) | Select vehicle. Info panel: type, condition, assigned operator, current task, fuel level. |
| Right-click vehicle | Context menu: Assign Operator, Send to Site, Send for Maintenance, Sell, Upgrade. |
| Click vehicle, then click destination | Send vehicle to that location. Path preview shows on road network. ETA displayed. |

**Why this design:** Vehicles are mobile assets that bridge sites. The send-to-location interaction is map-based and simple. Maintenance adds a management layer without being tedious.

**Pillars supported:** Build (logistics), Manage (fleet management).

#### Navigation Shortcuts

| Action | Behavior |
|--------|----------|
| Number keys 1-9 | Jump to saved camera positions (Ctrl+1-9 to save current position). |
| Home | Jump to company headquarters. |
| Ctrl+Home | Jump to active construction site (cycles if multiple active). |
| Tab | Cycle through active construction sites. |
| M | Open full world map. |
| Space (in orbit mode) | Quick pan to selected object. |

**Why this design:** At Tier 3+, the player manages multiple sites across a city. Fast navigation is essential. Numbered bookmarks and site cycling prevent "where was I?" frustration.

**Pillars supported:** All.

### 3.3 Controller Support

The game supports controllers with an adaptive interface:

- **Left stick:** Pan camera / move character (first-person)
- **Right stick:** Orbit / look (first-person)
- **Triggers:** Zoom in/out
- **D-pad:** Cycle through selections, navigate menus
- **A:** Confirm / select
- **B:** Cancel / back
- **X:** Context action
- **Y:** Toggle camera mode
- **Bumpers:** Cycle through active sites
- **Start:** Pause / main menu
- **Select:** Open world map

When a controller is detected, UI elements enlarge slightly, and radial menus replace some context menus for easier thumbstick navigation.

**Why this design:** Console players are a significant audience for simulation games. Controller support must be first-class, not an afterthought.

### 3.4 Undo/Redo System

- **Scope:** 50-action history per design session. Separate history per construction site.
- **Granularity:** Each object placement, deletion, modification, or material change is one action.
- **Group undo:** If the player drag-selects 10 walls and deletes them, Undo restores all 10 as one action.
- **Visual feedback:** Brief toast notification: "Undid: Wall moved 2m east."
- **Cost tracking:** Undoing a placed object refunds its full cost. Undoing a deletion re-charges the cost.
- **Cross-session:** Undo history persists until the building is completed and handed over. After handover, the building is permanent.

**Why this design:** Creative work requires fearlessness. Players must feel safe experimenting. The 50-action depth means they can try radical design changes and fully revert. The toast notification builds understanding of what just happened.

**Why this design:** Bidding is the strategic entry point to every contract. It's a minigame of risk/reward. The player must balance profit, feasibility, and competitiveness. The site inspection option adds spatial context to the numbers.

### 4.4 Site Inspection

**What happens when the player inspects a site:**
The camera flies to the plot. The plot is highlighted with property boundaries. The player sees:

- **Plot dimensions and area** (overlaid on ground)
- **Topography** (contour lines, slope percentage)
- **Soil type** (auto-detected: sandy, clay, loam, rocky)
- **Surrounding context** (neighboring buildings, roads, utilities, zoning boundaries)
- **Environmental factors** (sun path overlay, prevailing wind direction, flood zone if applicable)
- **Constraints** (setback requirements, height limits, easements, protected trees)

The player can:
- Toggle overlays on/off
- Use the measuring tool to check distances (click two points = distance line with measurement)
- Take drone photos for reference during design
- Place temporary markers for design ideas ("main entrance here", "parking here")
- View the city's zoning code summary for this plot

**Why this design:** Site inspection grounds the design process in reality. The player can't design a valid building without understanding the site. The soil type and topography directly affect foundation costs. The context affects design choices (don't block the neighbor's view, match the street's architectural rhythm).

### 4.5 Architecture Design

This is the deepest gameplay system. Detailed in Section 6. At the flow level:

**Entry point:** Player clicks "DESIGN" from dashboard or "Start Design" after winning a contract.

**What happens:**
1. The plot loads in the design environment. Clean, bright lighting. Grid overlay.
2. Client requirements appear as a persistent sidebar checklist.
3. The player designs the building using the full architectural toolset.
4. Real-time validation shows issues: "South wall exceeds height limit by 1.2m", "Bedroom 3 has no window — natural light requirement not met."
5. Cost estimate updates live as materials and dimensions change.
6. Player clicks "Submit for Approval" when satisfied.

**Duration:** 5-45 minutes depending on project complexity and player experience.

### 4.6 Planning & Preparation

**After design approval (by client), before construction begins:**

The player enters the Planning phase:

1. **Permit Management:** The game checks which permits are needed (building permit, electrical, plumbing, environmental, zoning variance if applicable). The player submits permit applications. Processing time varies (1-4 in-game weeks). Some permits can be expedited for a fee.

2. **Material Ordering:** A bill of materials is auto-generated from the design. The player reviews and can:
   - Accept all (default supplier, standard pricing)
   - Shop around (compare 3-5 suppliers per material, balance price/quality/delivery time)
   - Bulk order for discount (if multiple projects share materials)

3. **Crew Assignment:** The player assigns crews, subcontractors, and project managers to the project. Crew availability is visualized as a Gantt chart showing all active projects.

4. **Equipment Scheduling:** The player schedules equipment delivery (when does the excavator arrive? When is the crane needed?). Equipment conflicts across projects are flagged.

5. **Construction Schedule:** Auto-generated based on the design. The player can adjust sequencing (do foundation in March, framing in April, etc.) and see weather forecast integration (outdoor work blocked by forecast rain).

**Why this phase exists:** The Design/Plan/Build separation mirrors real construction. Planning is where management-oriented players shine — optimizing schedules, finding cost savings, preventing conflicts. It's also where players who over-designed learn about real-world constraints.

### 4.7 Construction

Detailed in Section 7. At the flow level:

1. Player clicks "BEGIN CONSTRUCTION" from the planning screen.
2. The game transitions to the Construction Site Camera mode.
3. Construction proceeds in phases. The player can:
   - **Watch and intervene:** Let crews work, step in when problems arise
   - **Manage proactively:** Adjust crew assignments, reorder materials, respond to weather
   - **Time control:** Pause, play, fast-forward (up to 10x for quick builds)
4. Milestone notifications: "Foundation Complete", "Frame Raised", "Roof On", "Interior Finishes Started"
5. Construction complete → moves to Inspection phase

### 4.8 Inspection

**What happens:**
A city inspector arrives (NPC with personality — some are strict, some are lenient based on your reputation and relationship). The player walks them through the building in first-person or guides them via orbit mode.

The inspector checks each system:
- Structural (foundation, framing, load paths)
- Electrical (wiring, panel, outlets)
- Plumbing (pipes, fixtures, water pressure)
- Fire safety (alarms, sprinklers, exits)
- Accessibility (ramps, door widths, bathroom clearances)
- Energy (insulation, HVAC, window ratings)
- Final finish quality (paint, trim, flooring)

Each check results in: Pass / Conditional Pass (minor fixes needed) / Fail (major rework required).

**If failed:** The player must address the issue (send crew back, pay for rework) and schedule re-inspection. This costs time and money and may cause deadline pressure.

**If passed:** Celebration moment. The inspector hands over the certificate. The client is notified.

### 4.9 Delivery

**Client Walkthrough:**
The client arrives. First-person walkthrough (player can accompany or let it auto-play). The client reacts to each room based on quality and alignment with their preferences. Emotional reactions visible (smiling, nodding, frowning at issues).

**Final Payment:**
Payment processed. Base contract value + bonuses (early completion, quality, client satisfaction) - penalties (late, failed inspections, client dissatisfaction).

**Portfolio Update:**
Building added to portfolio. Professional photos auto-generated from best angles. Player can add custom photos. The building appears in the world permanently.

**Review:**
Client leaves a star rating (1-5) and written review. Good reviews appear on future contract cards. Bad reviews hurt reputation.

### 4.10 Company Management

Detailed in Section 8. Between contracts, or whenever the player chooses, they enter management mode:

- Review financials
- Hire/fire/train employees
- Purchase/sell/upgrade equipment
- Expand to new regions
- Pursue research and technology unlocks
- Respond to events (economic changes, competitor actions, city council decisions)
- Set company policies (safety standards, sustainability commitments, wage levels)

## 5. TIME SYSTEM

### 5.1 Core Time Rules

| Rule | Value |
|------|-------|
| Default game speed | 1 real second = 1 in-game hour (1:3600 ratio at 1x) |
| 1 in-game day | 24 in-game hours = 24 real seconds at 1x |
| 1 in-game week | 7 days = ~2.8 real minutes at 1x |
| 1 in-game month | 30 days = ~12 real minutes at 1x |
| 1 in-game year | 365 days = ~2.4 real hours at 1x |

### 5.2 Speed Controls

| Speed | Ratio | Use Case |
|-------|-------|----------|
| Pause (Space) | 0:1 | Reading, planning, designing, emergencies |
| 1x | 1:3600 | Watching a critical construction moment, walkthroughs |
| 3x | 1:1200 | Standard construction monitoring |
| 5x | 1:720 | "Get through this phase" speed |
| 10x | 1:360 | Waiting for permits, material delivery, overnight |
| Max (fast-forward to event) | Variable | Jump to next milestone/event |

**Speed change behavior:** Gradual transition (0.5 second ramp) to prevent disorientation. Current speed shown as small indicator in corner. Speed auto-pauses on: new event notification, problem requiring player input, inspection failure.

### 5.3 Working Hours

- **Standard workday:** 07:00 - 17:00 (10 hours with breaks)
- **Overtime:** 17:00 - 21:00 (costs 1.5x labor rate, increases fatigue)
- **Night work:** 21:00 - 07:00 (costs 2x labor rate, requires permits in most zones, doubles fatigue, some tasks impossible: noisy excavation after 20:00)
- **Weekends:** Saturday optional (overtime rate), Sunday off by default (can schedule at 2x cost with significant morale penalty)

**Why this design:** Time constraints create meaningful decisions. "Do we push overtime to meet the deadline?" is a classic construction dilemma. The cost/fatigue/morale tradeoffs make these choices interesting rather than automatic.

### 5.4 Calendar & Seasons

The game tracks a full calendar year. Seasons affect construction:

| Season | Months (Northern Hemisphere) | Construction Effects |
|--------|------------------------------|---------------------|
| Spring | March - May | Mild weather, occasional rain delays. Good for starting projects. |
| Summer | June - August | Hot = faster concrete curing, but worker fatigue +30%. Thunderstorms in some regions. |
| Autumn | September - November | Ideal building weather. High contractor demand = higher labor costs. |
| Winter | December - February | Cold = slower concrete curing, frozen ground = excavation delays. Snow blocks outdoor work. Indoor finishing unaffected. |

**Seasonal events:**
- Spring: Construction industry conference (networking, new contracts)
- Summer: Student intern applications (cheap temporary labor)
- Autumn: Annual awards season (reputation opportunities)
- Winter: Holiday shutdown (1-2 weeks of no work, plan accordingly)

**Why this design:** Seasons create natural variety in the gameplay rhythm. Players learn to schedule projects around seasons — start foundations in spring, do framing in summer, finish interiors in winter. This is authentic to real construction and creates strategic depth.

### 5.5 Weather System

Weather is region-specific and forecastable (7-day forecast always visible during planning/construction).

| Weather | Effect |
|---------|--------|
| Clear | Normal operations. |
| Light Rain | Outdoor work continues at 80% speed. |
| Heavy Rain | Outdoor work stops. Flooding risk in excavations. |
| Thunderstorm | All work stops. Risk of equipment damage. Crews go home. |
| Snow | Outdoor work stops. Material delivery delayed. |
| Extreme Heat (>35°C/95°F) | Work continues at 60% speed. Mandatory breaks. Heat illness risk. |
| Extreme Cold (<-10°C/14°F) | Concrete cannot be poured. Most outdoor work impossible. |
| High Wind (>40 km/h) | Crane operations suspended. Roofing impossible. |

**Player response options:**
- Tarp/protect exposed work
- Reschedule weather-sensitive tasks
- Shift crew to interior work
- Accept delay (cheapest, but timeline impact)

### 5.6 Deadlines

Every contract has a deadline. The deadline is displayed:
- On the active contracts dashboard (countdown: "3 months, 12 days remaining")
- In the construction site HUD
- As a notification at 75%, 90%, and 95% of the timeline consumed

**Missing a deadline:**
- Financial penalty (typically 5-15% of contract value, scaled by lateness)
- Reputation penalty to Reliability score
- Client relationship damage (unlikely to hire you again)
- In extreme cases (2x+ deadline exceeded): contract cancellation, legal action, major reputation hit

**Beating a deadline:**
- Early completion bonus (defined in contract, typically 5-10%)
- Reliability reputation gain
- Client delight (higher chance of direct re-hire)

## 6. BUILDING DESIGN WORKFLOW

### 6.1 Design Entry

Player clicks "DESIGN" from contract view or dashboard. The game transitions:

- Screen fades to the design environment
- The plot is centered. Clean white grid lines on the ground. Subtle ambient lighting.
- Client requirements panel appears on the right side of the screen (persistent, collapsible)
- Design toolbar appears on the left (vertical, icon-based with text labels on hover)
- Top bar shows: Project name, current cost estimate, square footage, room count, save status

### 6.2 Design Tool Sequence

#### Step 1: Survey & Measurements

**What the player sees:** The plot with boundary lines, contour topography overlay, property line dimensions. A measuring tool cursor.

**What the player can do:**
- Click to place measurement points
- See distances between any two clicked points
- Measure from property line to potential building edge (for setback compliance)
- View sun path overlay (time of day slider to check shadows)
- View wind rose (prevailing wind direction)

**Auto-generated data:** The game automatically calculates buildable area (total plot minus required setbacks), maximum footprint, and maximum height from zoning data.

**Cost estimate:** Survey is free. It's information gathering.

**Why this step exists:** In real construction, you don't just start drawing walls. The site dictates what's possible. This step teaches players to work within constraints — a core skill that scales to complex projects.

#### Step 2: Terrain Editing (Optional)

**What the player sees:** The plot in 3D with contour lines. A terrain modification toolbar.

**What the player can do:**
- Flatten areas (cost: excavation + soil removal)
- Raise areas (cost: fill + compaction)
- Create retaining walls (cost by linear meter + height)
- Add drainage features

**Constraints:** Terrain modification costs are shown live. Major modifications may require additional permits (flagged by the game).

**Why this step exists:** Real sites aren't perfectly flat. Terrain editing is optional but important — building on a slope without a retaining wall will fail inspection. Players learn to work with terrain or pay to modify it.

#### Step 3: Foundation

**What the player sees:** A foundation placement tool. Options: slab-on-grade, crawl space, full basement.

**What the player can do:**
- Select foundation type (each has different costs, suitability for soil/terrain)
- Define foundation footprint by drawing a polygon on the terrain
- Adjust foundation depth (affects basement height, excavation cost)
- Add foundation elements: footings, frost walls, drainage, waterproofing

**Auto-validation:**
- "Slab foundation on slope >5%: Not recommended. Consider stepped foundation or terrain modification."
- "Basement below water table level: Requires enhanced waterproofing (+$12,000)."

**Cost estimate:** Updates live as foundation dimensions change.

#### Step 4: Structural Grid & Load-Bearing Elements

**What the player sees:** A grid overlay on the foundation. Column placement tool. Load-bearing wall distinction.

**What the player can do:**
- Place columns (structural grid auto-snaps)
- Define load-bearing walls (shown with thicker outline, different color in blueprint view)
- Set floor-to-floor heights
- Define structural system (timber frame, steel frame, concrete, hybrid)

**Auto-design option:** For players who don't want to engineer the structure, an "Auto-Structure" button generates a structurally sound layout based on the building dimensions. The player can then modify it.

**Why load-bearing matters:** This is where the game teaches that walls aren't just dividers — some hold the building up. Removing a load-bearing wall triggers an immediate warning: "This wall is load-bearing. Removing it requires adding a beam or column (+$4,200)." This creates authentic architectural decision-making.

#### Step 5: Walls

**What the player sees:** Wall drawing tool. Cursor shows wall preview with dimensions.

**What the player can do:**
- Draw walls by clicking start point and end point
- Draw walls by click-dragging
- Draw rectangular rooms with the Room tool (click-drag creates 4 walls)
- Adjust wall thickness (interior: 10-15cm, exterior: 20-30cm depending on insulation)
- Set wall type: Interior, Exterior, Load-bearing Interior, Party Wall, Fire Wall
- Adjust wall height (room height)
- Curved walls (for premium designs)

**Snapping:** Walls snap to:
- Grid (default 10cm, configurable)
- Other wall endpoints and midpoints
- Column positions
- Perpendicular/parallel alignment to existing walls

**Live validation:**
- Wall too close to property line → red highlight + setback violation warning
- Wall exceeds height limit → red highlight + zoning violation warning
- Wall creates room with no door access → yellow warning ("Room will have no entrance")
- Wall blocks required window placement → yellow warning

#### Step 6: Rooms

**What the player sees:** As walls enclose spaces, the game auto-detects and labels rooms. A room list populates in the sidebar.

**What the player can do:**
- Click any room to select it (walls highlight)
- Assign room type from a categorized list: Bedroom, Bathroom, Kitchen, Living Room, Dining Room, Hallway, Closet, Utility, Office, Retail, Storage, Garage, etc.
- See room area (m²) update live
- Set room-specific properties (e.g., Bathroom requires waterproofing, Kitchen requires ventilation)

**Client requirements tracking:** The requirements panel shows:
- "Minimum 2 bedrooms: ✓ (3 built)"
- "Minimum 1 full bathroom: ✓ (2 built)"
- "Kitchen minimum 12 m²: ✗ (currently 10.5 m²)"

**Why room auto-detection:** Reduces busywork. Players focus on wall placement; the game handles room identification. Room type assignment is the meaningful gameplay — meeting client requirements, optimizing layout, balancing room sizes.

#### Step 7: Doors and Windows

**What the player sees:** Door and window placement mode. Library of door types (single, double, sliding, pocket, garage, French) and window types (fixed, casement, awning, bay, skylight, curtain wall).

**What the player can do:**
- Click on a wall to place a door/window
- Drag to position along the wall
- Adjust width and height
- Set swing direction for doors (left/right, in/out)
- Set window sill height
- Place door/window schedules (for repetitive placement across floors)

**Validation:**
- Door too narrow for accessibility requirement → warning
- Window area insufficient for natural light requirement → warning
- Exterior door without steps/ramp → warning
- Window on property-line-adjacent wall → privacy/fire code warning (varies by jurisdiction)

#### Step 8: Floors and Stairs

**What the player sees:** Floor management panel. The building can have multiple floors stacked vertically. Each floor is a separate design layer.

**What the player can do:**
- Add floor above/below current floor
- Set floor-to-floor height per level
- Copy floor layout to new floor (with option to mirror)
- Place stairs (straight, L-shaped, U-shaped, spiral)
- Place elevator shaft (required for buildings over 4 stories in most codes)
- Navigate between floors using the floor selector or Page Up/Page Down

**Multi-floor view:** The player can toggle between:
- Single floor view (current floor only, others dimmed)
- Stacked view (ghost outlines of other floors visible for alignment)
- Section view (cut-through showing all floors in profile)

#### Step 9: Roof

**What the player sees:** Roof design tool. The building's top floor walls are highlighted as the roof base.

**What the player can do:**
- Select roof type: Flat, Gable, Hip, Mansard, Gambrel, Shed, Butterfly, Curved
- Adjust roof pitch (angle in degrees, 0°-60°)
- Set overhang distance
- Add dormers
- Place skylights
- Select roofing material (asphalt shingle, metal, tile, slate, green/living roof)

**Auto-roof option:** Generates a standard gable or hip roof that fits the building footprint. Player can then customize.

**Validation:**
- Roof height counted toward total building height (zoning limit check)
- Roof pitch too low for snow region → warning (structural concern)
- Flat roof requires drainage plan

#### Step 10: Utilities (MEP — Mechanical, Electrical, Plumbing)

**What the player sees:** A toggleable overlay showing utility routing. Color-coded: blue (water supply), red (hot water), yellow (gas), green (electrical), orange (data), gray (HVAC ducts), brown (sewage/waste).

**What the player can do:**
- Place main utility connections (where services enter the building)
- Route main lines (auto-routing available, manual routing for optimization)
- Place electrical panel(s)
- Place HVAC equipment (furnace, AC, heat pump, ERV)
- Place water heater
- Set ventilation requirements per room

**Auto-MEP option:** For players who don't want to design utilities, the game auto-generates code-compliant MEP. Manual MEP design is for optimization and cost savings.

**Why MEP exists as a design step:** This is where the game teaches that buildings are more than walls and roofs. The "guts" of a building affect livability, energy efficiency, and inspection compliance. It's optional depth for engaged players and auto-solved for others.

#### Step 11: Finishes & Fixtures (Interior Design)

**What the player sees:** Interior design mode. The building becomes semi-transparent or the roof hides. Each room is a canvas.

**What the player can do:**
- Select flooring per room (hardwood, tile, carpet, vinyl, concrete, etc.)
- Select wall finishes (paint color, wallpaper, tile, wainscoting)
- Select ceiling finish
- Place fixtures: toilets, sinks, bathtubs, showers, kitchen counters, cabinets, closets
- Place lighting: ceiling lights, wall sconces, recessed lighting, track lighting
- Place appliances: refrigerator, stove, dishwasher, washer/dryer
- Set trim style (baseboards, crown molding, door/window casing)

**Furniture placement (optional):** For showcase builds, the player can place furniture. This is optional — the contract typically only requires fixtures, not furniture.

**Template rooms:** Common room configurations are available as one-click templates (e.g., "Standard Bathroom: toilet, sink, shower, tile floor, vanity light").

#### Step 12: Exterior Finishes

**What the player sees:** Exterior view of the building. Material palette panel.

**What the player can do:**
- Select facade material per wall section (brick, stucco, wood siding, fiber cement, metal panel, stone veneer, glass curtain wall)
- Select color/finish for each material
- Add architectural details: cornices, columns, shutters, window boxes, balcony railings
- Design landscaping: lawn, trees, shrubs, flower beds, hardscape (walkways, patios, driveways)
- Place exterior lighting
- Add fencing or property boundary markers

#### Step 13: Validation

**What the player sees:** A comprehensive validation report.

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ DESIGN VALIDATION REPORT                     │
│ Riverside Apartments                         │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ ✅ Structural: All load paths resolved       │
│ ✅ Zoning: Within height and setback limits │

│ ✅ Accessibility: ADA/EN standards met       │
│ ✅ Energy: KfW 40 requirements met           │
│ ⚠️ Natural light: Unit 3B below minimum     │
│ ⚠️ Parking: Only 22 of 24 required spaces   │
│ ❌ Client requirement: 4 retail not met (3) │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ ESTIMATED COST: $3,420,000                   │
│ CLIENT BUDGET: $3,200,000 - $4,800,000       │
│ ESTIMATED DURATION: 15 months                │
│ PROFIT MARGIN: 15.8% (at bid price)          │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**What the player can do:**
- Click any issue to jump to the problem location
- Fix issues manually or click "Auto-Fix" for individual issues
- "Auto-Fix All" resolves all warnings and errors (may change the design significantly)
- "Override" some warnings if the player believes the design is correct and wants to argue the case during permitting

#### Step 14: Cost Estimate

Auto-generated from the complete design. Broken down by:
- Site preparation
- Foundation
- Structure
- Envelope (walls, windows, doors, roof)
- Interior finishes
- MEP systems
- Exterior/Landscaping
- Permits and fees
- Labor (estimated from similar projects)
- Contingency (default 10%, adjustable)

#### Step 15: Blueprint & Client Approval

**What the player sees:** A set of generated blueprint views:
- Floor plans (each floor)
- Elevations (4 sides)
- Section view
- Site plan
- 3D render (exterior)
- Interior renders (key rooms)

**What the player can do:**
- Review each sheet
- Add annotations
- Export as image for sharing
- Click "Submit to Client"

**Client response (after ~1 in-game day):**
- **Approved:** Move to Planning phase. Client may add minor revision requests that don't block approval.
- **Revision Requested:** Client wants changes. Specific requests listed: "Master bedroom too small — minimum 18 m² please." Player revises and resubmits.

**Why this step exists:** The approval step creates consequence for the design phase. You can't just build whatever you want — the client has expectations. Revision requests are realistic and teach players to read requirements carefully.

## 7. CONSTRUCTION WORKFLOW

### 7.1 Construction Entry

After planning is complete, the player clicks "BEGIN CONSTRUCTION." The game transitions to the active construction site.

### 7.2 Construction Phases (Automatic Sequencing)

Construction follows a fixed phase order (mirroring real construction):

```
Phase 1: SITE PREP         (5-10% of total duration)
├── Surveying & staking
├── Site clearing (trees, debris)
├── Temporary utilities (power, water for site)
├── Erosion control
└── Excavation

Phase 2: FOUNDATION        (10-15%)
├── Footings (concrete pour, cure time)
├── Foundation walls
├── Waterproofing
├── Drainage
└── Foundation inspection ← CRITICAL GATE (fail = redo)

Phase 3: STRUCTURE          (20-25%)
├── Framing/structural steel
├── Floor joists and decking
├── Structural inspection ← CRITICAL GATE
└── "Topping out" milestone (building reaches full height)

Phase 4: ENVELOPE           (15-20%)
├── Exterior sheathing
├── Windows and exterior doors
├── Roofing
├── Weather barrier/House wrap
└── Building is now "dried in" (weather-protected)

Phase 5: MEP ROUGH-IN       (15-20%)
├── Electrical rough-in (wiring, panels)
├── Plumbing rough-in (pipes, drains)
├── HVAC rough-in (ductwork)
├── Data/low voltage
└── MEP rough-in inspection ← CRITICAL GATE

Phase 6: INTERIOR FINISHES  (20-25%)
├── Insulation
├── Drywall (hang, tape, mud, sand)
├── Painting
├── Flooring
├── Trim and millwork
├── Cabinetry and countertops
└── Interior inspection ← CRITICAL GATE

Phase 7: FINAL SYSTEMS      (10-15%)
├── Fixture installation (lights, plumbing fixtures, switches)
├── Appliance installation
├── HVAC final (thermostats, registers)
├── Fire safety final (alarms, sprinkler test)
└── Systems test (everything turned on simultaneously)

Phase 8: FINISHING & PUNCH  (5-10%)
├── Final cleaning
├── Punch list (minor fixes)
├── Landscaping completion
├── Final inspection ← FINAL GATE
└── Certificate of Occupancy
```

### 7.3 Player's Role During Construction

The player is the CEO/Project Manager, NOT the laborer. The player does NOT:
- Swing hammers
- Pour concrete
- Install drywall
- Wire outlets

The player DOES:
- Monitor progress and quality
- Respond to problems and decisions
- Re-allocate resources
- Approve or reject completed work
- Communicate with the client about changes
- Handle unexpected events

### 7.4 What the AI Handles

Construction crews work autonomously:
- Laborers move to assigned zones and perform tasks
- Task completion is based on crew size, skill level, equipment, and conditions
- Crews take scheduled breaks automatically
- Crews go home at end of workday
- Material usage is automatic (crews draw from stockpiles)

### 7.5 Automation Levels

| Level | Name | Description |
|-------|------|-------------|
| 0 | Manual | Player must approve every phase transition and resolve every issue. Full control. |
| 1 | Semi-Auto | Crews auto-proceed through phases. Player notified of issues only. Recommended for Tier 1-3. |
| 2 | Managed | Project Manager NPC handles day-to-day. Player only sees major issues and critical gates. Requires hiring a Project Manager. Tier 3+ feature. |
| 3 | Delegated | Regional VP handles everything. Player sees summary reports only. Tier 5+ feature. |

**Why automation levels exist:** Early game, the player wants to be hands-on with their first few builds. Late game, managing 10+ simultaneous sites manually would be miserable. Automation is an earned convenience that reflects the player's growing company.

### 7.6 Problems and Interventions

Construction is not a waiting game. Problems arise that require player decisions:

| Problem | Trigger | Player Decision |
|---------|---------|-----------------|
| Material delay | Supplier late shipment | Wait (no cost) / Expedite from alternate supplier (+15% cost) / Substitute material (quality risk) |
| Crew shortage | Workers sick/quitting | Hire temps (lower quality) / Pull from another project (delays there) / Work overtime (fatigue) |
| Weather event | Forecast storm | Tarp and protect (+$2K) / Risk it (potential damage) / Pause outdoor work (timeline delay) |
| Design conflict | Blueprint doesn't match reality | Modify design on-site (cost + minor quality hit) / Stop and redesign properly (delay) |
| Inspection failure | Work below standard | Rework (time + cost) / Argue with inspector (relationship risk) / Bribe (reputation + legal risk, Veteran difficulty only) |
| Client change request | Client wants modification | Accept (timeline + cost adjustment, client pays or you absorb) / Decline (client satisfaction hit) |
| Safety incident | Worker injured | First aid on site / Hospital transport (+cost) / Report to authorities (mandatory for serious) |
| Equipment breakdown | Machine fails | Repair (time + cost) / Replace (higher cost, faster) / Rent temporary (mid cost) |

### 7.7 Delay Visualization

When construction falls behind schedule:
- The timeline bar turns yellow (slight delay) to orange (moderate) to red (critical)
- The site manager NPC provides commentary: "We're 3 days behind on framing. The rain last week really set us back."
- The client may call/email expressing concern
- Financial projection updates to show potential penalty

### 7.8 Quality System

Quality is tracked per construction zone (0-100%). It starts at 0 and increases as work completes.

**Factors affecting final quality:**
- Worker skill level (apprentice = max 70%, journeyman = max 85%, master = max 100%)
- Material grade (budget = base, standard = +5%, premium = +10%)
- Weather during construction (perfect conditions = +5%, adverse = -10%)
- Rushed work (overtime/night work = -5% per phase)
- Equipment condition (well-maintained = base, poor = -5%)
- Inspection rigor (strict inspector may find issues others miss)

**Quality impact:**
- 90-100%: "Exceptional." Client delighted. Portfolio showcase. Reputation gain.
- 75-89%: "Good." Standard result. Client satisfied.
- 60-74%: "Adequate." Client accepts but not impressed. Minor reputation hit.
- Below 60%: "Poor." Client may refuse payment. Inspection likely failed. Reputation damage.

### 7.9 Satisfying Progress

Construction progress satisfaction comes from:

1. **Visual transformation:** The building visibly grows. Players can see framing rise, walls appear, windows go in. Each phase is visually distinct.

2. **Milestone celebrations:** Critical phase completions trigger a brief celebration: crew high-fives, a small animation, a satisfying "thunk" sound as the final beam is placed. The milestone is recorded in the project timeline.

3. **Time-lapse replay:** After construction completes, the player can watch a 30-second time-lapse of their entire build from foundation to finished building. This is immensely satisfying and highly shareable.

4. **Before/After comparison:** The game auto-generates a side-by-side showing the empty plot and the finished building from the same angle. This is added to the portfolio.

5. **Client reaction:** Seeing the client's emotional response during the walkthrough provides social validation. A delighted client is a reward in itself.

## 8. COMPANY MANAGEMENT WORKFLOW

### 8.1 Employee Management

**Hiring:**
The player posts job openings through the HR panel. Candidates appear over 1-4 in-game days. Each candidate has:
- Name, age, photo (generated character)
- Role and skill level (Apprentice/Journeyman/Master)
- Salary expectation
- Special traits (e.g., "Fast Learner," "Natural Leader," "Perfectionist," "Gets Sick Often")
- Employment history
- Personality description

Player selects candidates and makes offers. Candidates may accept, negotiate, or decline based on salary, company reputation, and competing offers.

**Managing:**
- Assign workers to projects/crews
- Set individual wages (within role band)
- Promote high performers (requires skill certification and pay raise)
- Discipline underperformers (warning → probation → termination)
- Approve training requests
- Grant bonuses for exceptional work
- Handle personal issues (worker requests time off, has conflict with coworker)

**Losing workers:**
- Resignation (better offer, moving, retirement)
- Termination (performance, misconduct)
- Poaching (competitors may recruit your best people)
- Injury/incapacity (safety incidents)

**Crew System:**
Workers are organized into crews (3-10 workers + 1 crew lead). Crews stay together across projects, building teamwork bonuses over time. Breaking up a long-standing crew causes a temporary morale penalty.

### 8.2 Contract Management

**Active contracts:** Dashboard shows all in-progress work with status, timeline, budget tracking, and quick actions.

**Contract negotiation:** For invitation-only and direct-hire contracts, the player can negotiate terms:
- Higher budget (in exchange for tighter timeline or higher quality commitment)
- Extended timeline (in exchange for lower price)
- Reduced scope (client drops optional requirements for discount)

**Contract disputes:** If client and builder disagree about scope or quality, a dispute process begins:
- Negotiation (free, takes time)
- Mediation (costs money, neutral third party)
- Legal action (expensive, reputation impact, last resort)

### 8.3 Fleet & Equipment Management

**Vehicle fleet:**
- Purchase new (full price) or used (lower price, higher maintenance)
- Assign vehicles to projects or keep in pool
- Schedule maintenance (preventive = lower failure risk)
- Upgrade vehicles (better engine, larger capacity)
- Sell/trade-in when upgrading

**Heavy equipment:**
- Excavators, bulldozers, cranes, concrete pumps, etc.
- Own (high upfront, lower per-use cost) or rent (no upfront, higher per-use)
- Equipment requires certified operators (separate from general laborers)
- Equipment has utilization tracking — idle equipment costs money

### 8.5 Loans & Financing

### 9.2 Notification Philosophy

**The game will NOT spam the player.** Notifications follow strict rules:

1. **Batching:** Multiple minor events in 5 minutes are batched into one summary: "3 tasks completed, 2 materials delivered, 1 worker returned from break."

2. **Context awareness:** During active construction (player watching live), notifications are suppressed because the player can see what's happening. During dashboard/management mode, notifications appear.

3. **Priority filtering:** Only events relevant to the player's current focus appear. When designing a building, construction site notifications from other projects are queued and delivered in a batch when the player returns to management mode.

4. **Player-configurable:** In settings, players can set notification preferences per category and per urgency level.

**Text scaling:** UI text scales from 100% to 200% in 10% increments. All UI layouts accommodate scaling without breaking.

**High contrast mode:** Increases contrast of UI elements. Reduces background transparency. Adds outlines to interactive elements.

**Screen reader support:** All UI text is exposed to screen readers. Critical game events are announced.

### 13.2 Motor Accessibility

## 15. EDGE CASES

### 15.1 Bankruptcy

**Trigger:** Cash reserves zero OR negative, no available credit, no active contracts generating income.

**Game response:**
- Apprentice/Professional difficulty: "Restructuring" — lose 50% of assets, reset to Tier 2, keep skills and reputation. Mentor Pllana provides a small emergency loan.
- Veteran difficulty: "Receivership" — lose all assets, keep only skills. Start over at Tier 1 in a different city. Former company becomes an AI competitor.

**Player experience:** A sober moment, not a rage-quit. The game frames it as a learning experience. Historical data shows what went wrong. "Here's where the cash flow turned negative. Here's where you could have cut costs or bid differently."

### 15.2 No Workers Available

**Trigger:** All workers assigned to projects, no one available for new task/emergency.

**Game response:** Temporary labor option appears — hire day laborers at 2x cost. Lower quality, but fills the gap. Alternatively, the player can pull workers from less critical projects.

### 15.3 Extreme Weather

**Trigger:** Hurricane, blizzard, flood warning for active construction site.

**Game response:**
- Alert triggers immediate
- Player options:
  - **Evacuate site** (safest — workers safe, equipment moved, but 3-5 day delay)
  - **Weather-proof and continue** (protect what you can, accept some damage risk)
  - **Ignore warning** (workers may refuse, safety reputation nosedives, high damage risk)
- Post-event: assess damage, file insurance claims, repair timeline impact

### 15.4 Equipment Failure

**Trigger:** Equipment condition drops below threshold OR random failure event (probability scales with condition and age).

**Game response:**
- Work in that zone pauses
- Repair options: Call repair service (1-3 day wait, moderate cost) / Emergency repair (same day, 3x cost) / Replace (buy new, immediate if in stock)
- If owned: repair cost is player's responsibility
- If rented: rental company provides replacement (but may take time)
- Ripple effect: tasks dependent on equipment delayed

### 15.5 Material Shortages

**Trigger:** Supplier cannot fulfill order (random economic event, or player ordered insufficient quantity).

**Game response:**
- Notification with options:
  - Source from alternate supplier (usually higher cost)
  - Substitute with different material (may affect quality or inspection)
  - Wait for restock (timeline delay)
  - Reduce scope (negotiate with client to eliminate element requiring that material)

### 15.6 Failed Inspections

**Trigger:** Work quality below threshold for the inspecting jurisdiction.

**Game response:**
- Inspector provides written report listing specific failures
- Player must fix issues (send crew back, order rework)
- Re-inspection scheduled (1-5 day wait, fee applies)
- Multiple failures on same project: inspector becomes more strict, fees increase
- Relationship with inspector degrades (future projects may face tougher scrutiny)

### 15.7 Contract Cancellation

**Client cancels:** Rare. Occurs if client goes bankrupt, project funding falls through, or client becomes extremely dissatisfied.

**Player response:**
- Partial payment for work completed (if contract includes cancellation clause)
- Reputation impact (cancelled contracts appear on company record)
- Building remains partially constructed (player owns it unless contract specifies otherwise)
- Options: finish at own expense and sell, demolish, or leave as-is

**Player cancels:** Player can withdraw from a contract, but:
- Reputation penalty (significant — "unreliable builder")
- May owe damages to client
- May be sued (legal costs)
- Only advisable in extreme circumstances

### 15.8 Worker Strikes

**Trigger:** Worker morale drops below 20% for extended period, OR specific trigger event (mass layoffs, wage cuts, safety incidents).

**Game response:**
- Workers refuse to work
- Picketing at company HQ and active sites (visual)
- Negotiation required: player meets with worker representative
- Resolution options: meet demands (higher wages, better conditions), partial compromise, or hire replacement workers (extreme — reputation devastation, legal challenges)
- Strike duration varies based on player response

### 15.9 Client Bankruptcy

**Trigger:** Economic downturn or client-specific financial failure.

**Game response:**
- If project underway: construction pauses. Player files claim as creditor.
- Completed project may be sold at auction to recover debts.
- Player may not receive full payment (partial recovery from bankruptcy proceedings).
- Reputation impact: none (client failure, not builder fault).

## 16. OPEN DESIGN QUESTIONS

### Question 1: Should the game include multiplayer at launch?

**Option A:** Launch with single-player only, add co-op multiplayer post-launch.
**Option B:** Include 2-4 player co-op at launch.

**Recommendation:** Option A — Launch single-player only.

**Justification:** The core game is a deep single-player experience. Multiplayer adds enormous complexity (synchronized simulation states, shared economy, conflict resolution between players). Adding multiplayer at launch would delay the game by 12-18 months and risk the quality of the single-player experience, which is what 95%+ of players will primarily engage with. Post-launch, multiplayer can be developed with real player data and community feedback.

### Question 2: Should there be a stock market / public company mechanic?

**Option A:** Company can go public at Tier 5+, adding shareholder demands, quarterly earnings pressure, stock price, and hostile takeover risk.
**Option B:** Company stays private throughout. No public market mechanics.

**Recommendation:** Option A — Public company at Tier 5+.

**Justification:** Going public is a natural milestone for a corporation reaching national/global scale. It adds meaningful late-game gameplay (shareholder meetings, earnings calls, takeover defense) that differentiates Tier 5-6 from earlier tiers. The mechanic makes late-game management feel different from mid-game rather than just "more of the same but bigger." However, it should be optional — players who prefer private ownership can decline the IPO.

### Question 3: Should buildings degrade over time?

**Option A:** Completed buildings slowly degrade (maintenance required on owned buildings).
**Option B:** Completed buildings are permanent and unchanging.

**Recommendation:** Option B with a light Option A variant.

**Justification:** Full degradation adds tedious maintenance gameplay that doesn't serve any of the four pillars. However, for buildings the player retains ownership of (rental properties, company HQ), a light maintenance system adds realism and occasional decision points ("Invest $50K in HQ renovation or let it age?"). Buildings owned by clients are their responsibility, not the player's.

### Question 4: How should blueprints work across saves?

**Option A:** Blueprints are save-specific. Each new career starts from scratch.
**Option B:** Blueprints are account-wide. Designs from any save can be imported.

**Recommendation:** Option B — Account-wide blueprints.

**Justification:** If a player spends 45 minutes designing a perfect apartment building, they should be able to reuse it in future playthroughs. The Creative Architect mode exists specifically for this. It rewards design investment and creates a personal library that grows over time. Community blueprint sharing (post-launch) builds on this foundation.

### Question 5: Should construction accidents include worker fatalities?

**Option A:** Safety incidents can result in worker death (realistic, high emotional stakes).
**Option B:** Safety incidents result only in injuries (maintains E rating, less traumatic).

**Recommendation:** Option B — Injuries only.

**Justification:** The game targets an E for Everyone rating and aims to be relaxing and satisfying. Worker death would fundamentally change the emotional tone from "satisfying builder" to "high-stakes simulator." Serious injuries provide sufficient consequence and emotional weight without crossing into traumatic territory. This also avoids potential PR issues.

### Question 6: Should there be a story campaign with a defined ending?

**Option A:** Full narrative campaign with scripted events, character arcs, and a definitive ending.
**Option B:** Open-ended sandbox with emergent storytelling, no scripted ending.

**Recommendation:** Hybrid — scripted opening, emergent thereafter.

**Justification:** A fully scripted campaign contradicts the open-ended "build your empire" fantasy. However, a scripted opening (Mentor Pllana's guidance through Tier 1) provides essential onboarding and emotional grounding. After Tier 2, the story becomes fully emergent — driven by the player's choices, the living city, and the Legacy Milestone system. The "ending" is player-defined through Legacy Milestones.

### Question 7: Should time pass during the design phase?

**Option A:** Design happens in "design time" — game time does not advance while designing.
**Option B:** Game time advances during design (deadlines tick while you're designing).

**Recommendation:** Option A — Design time is paused.

**Justification:** Rushing players through the creative phase is anti-fun. Design is where players express themselves. Time pressure during design would push players toward rushed, generic buildings. The trade-off is realism sacrifice, but the gameplay benefit (creative freedom without anxiety) is worth it. The time cost of design is reflected in the project timeline (a complex design takes longer to build), which is a more interesting mechanic than "the clock is ticking while you think."

### Question 8: Can the player build structures they own and operate (not for clients)?

**Option A:** Player can build and retain properties — rental income, company facilities, speculative development.
**Option B:** All construction is for clients. Player only builds what's contracted.

**Recommendation:** Option A — Player can build for themselves.

**Justification:** This is a major progression milestone (unlocked at Tier 3). It transforms the player from a contractor into a developer. Building an apartment building you own, finding tenants, and collecting rent is a fundamentally different and satisfying gameplay loop. It also creates a passive income stream that reduces late-game grind. The player's own HQ is the ultimate personal project — designing the building your company operates from is a top emotional moment.
